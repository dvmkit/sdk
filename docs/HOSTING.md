# Hosted admission and draining

Hosting control is opt-in. Pass `hostingControl: { secret, scope }` to `createDVMHost` or `createDVMServer`, or set both `DVMKIT_HOSTING_CONTROL_SECRET` and `DVMKIT_HOSTING_CONTROL_SCOPE` for a hosted `createDVMHost` boot. Supply a dedicated random secret of at least 32 bytes, a stable deployment scope, `DVMKIT_DVM_ID`, and Postgres. A partial environment configuration fails boot. Do not reuse caller authentication keys, builder signing keys, or payment credentials. Configured scopes start closed: the management client must fund and sign a finite lease before the first payment. Without this option, self-hosted admission behaves as before and the control routes are absent.

The management client sends JSON to `POST /_hosting/control` with `action: "drain"` or `"resume"`. It sends JSON to `POST /_hosting/status` with `action: "status"`. Every body contains `scope`, `dvmId`, `timestampMs` (Unix milliseconds, within five minutes), and a fresh 16–128 character URL-safe `nonce`. A transition also contains a positive, monotonically increasing integer `generation`. Drain additionally contains `deadlineMs`, no more than 24 hours in the future. Drain and status keep the version-one signature: UTF-8 JSON of `["dvmkit-hosting-v1", action, scope, dvmId, generation ?? null, deadlineMs ?? null, timestampMs, nonce]`.

Resume **requires** `version: 2`, `leaseExpiresAtMs`, and `deadlineMs`. Both are Unix milliseconds. At the locked PostgreSQL time, the lease must be in the future and no more than one hour away; the completion deadline must be later than lease expiry and no more than one hour after it. The platform may use a shorter funded window, such as five minutes of admission and one hour for work already accepted. Version-two signed bytes are UTF-8 JSON of `["dvmkit-hosting-v2", action, scope, dvmId, generation ?? null, leaseExpiresAtMs ?? null, deadlineMs ?? null, timestampMs, nonce]`. Set `X-DVM-Hosting-Signature` to the lowercase hex HMAC-SHA256 returned by `signHostingControl(secret, body)` from `@dvmkit/sdk/server`. A captured nonce cannot be reused, even for a status read. Retry the identical generation, lease expiry, and completion deadline with a fresh nonce to get the current result while the lease remains valid. A stale generation, same-generation change, expired lease, or legacy unbounded resume returns 409.

The status body contains `state` (`accepting`, `draining`, or terminal `closed`), `generation`, `leaseExpiresAtMs` (or `null`), `deadlineMs` (or `null`), `activeJobIds`, `unownedActiveJobIds`, `pendingAdmissionCount`, `pendingAdmissions`, `unresolvedAdmissionCount`, `unresolvedAdmissions`, and `jobIdentityHeader`. `jobIdentityHeader` is `"v1"`: the deployment states each accepted job's ID in the `DVM-Job-Identity-V1` response-head field of its job submit, resume and request-result successes. `activeJobIds` contains only jobs admitted under the signed deployment scope. `unownedActiveJobIds` contains active jobs for the DVM ID that have no hosting admission under any scope, such as jobs created before hosting control was enabled. They are not automatically adopted or cancelled: another deployment may own them. Treat a nonempty unowned list as unresolved shutdown work and migrate each job only after identifying its deployment owner. Jobs admitted under another scope appear in neither list. The admission arrays carry durable admission and work IDs. All values come from Postgres and are shared by every instance. A pending admission was reserved before drain but has not finished verification or job persistence. An unresolved admission reached a payment verifier, which may have moved money, without a confirmed job. Treat it as recovery work; do not infer that no payment landed. When a matching job or funding record becomes durable, status resolves that admission. The existing payment and credit recovery paths remain available while draining.

New `/v1/job` submissions and fresh `/v1/credit` funding reserve admission under the scope row lock before payment verification. The SDK checks PostgreSQL time **after** obtaining that lock. Expiry atomically closes the gate and pins the completion deadline for admissions already in flight; a process restart or missed control call cannot reopen it. Existing accepting rows without a lease migrate closed and retain their accepted, pending, and unresolved work with a one-hour recovery deadline. An unleased row written by an older instance during a mixed-version rollout closes the same way when the new SDK next reads it. An already reserved submission may finish after admission closes; if a job persists after its deadline, the SDK cancels it before dispatch. Once the deadline passes, every instance sweeps scoped active persisted jobs to provider-attributed durable terminal cancellation, releases pending credit draws through the existing terminal path, emits a receipt when configured, and signals the running handler's `AbortSignal`. A later funded resume does not erase a prior run's deadline. Status, result, balance, refund, reclaim, and recovery of already recorded funding remain available. The one-second sweep cadence is not a healthy-job runtime limit; a job may run indefinitely while admission stays open under renewed leases.

A retry carrying the exact payment artifact of an accepted hosted job can recover that same job during drain without another rail verification. The SDK matches the artifact hash against its durable admission record, then checks the original request fingerprint and caller identity before returning the saved job token. A new artifact or changed request cannot use this recovery path. Signed public request-ID resume and recorded credit-funding replay also remain available.

Handlers should observe `ctx.signal` and stop their own work promptly. A handler can ignore abort, and an external API call may already have happened. Terminal cancellation prevents later SDK completion and keeps the caller's durable job and credit outcome truthful; it cannot undo effects outside the SDK. Reconcile any unresolved admission before shutting down the last machine that can observe its payment provider.

Terminal cleanup uses `POST /_hosting/closure` with `action: "close"`, then `POST /_hosting/closure-certificate` with `action: "certificate"`. Both commands require `version: 3`, the original `dvmId` and `scope`, the closure’s `generation`, a stable 16–128 character URL-safe `operationId`, a fresh `timestampMs` and `nonce`, and `X-DVM-Hosting-Signature`. The first close uses a generation strictly newer than the current control generation. Sign UTF-8 JSON of `["dvmkit-hosting-closure-v3", action, scope, dvmId, generation, operationId, timestampMs, nonce]` with HMAC-SHA256 using the hosting-control secret; `signHostingClosure` from `@dvmkit/sdk/server` produces the signature. A repeat close may reuse the same operation and generation with a fresh nonce. Closure is terminal for that scope: neither an older client nor a later lease can resume admission. The certificate response binds the original DVM and scope, persisted SDK instance ID and boot epoch, operation, generation, and database cutoff transaction. A restart changes the boot epoch and invalidates an older certificate.

`complete: true` and `eligible: true` require a sole managed mount, durable job/credit/replay/report stores, known rail stores, no pending or uncertain admission/payment/report/settlement, and zero caller balances and unresolved refund liabilities in every recorded currency. Unknown or unpriced facts refuse eligibility. Pending x402 payout batches also block. The SDK retains credit, job, and refund history and keeps recovery available after closure; a paid original Lightning invoice can still be credited or repaired under its persisted invoice and original admission. A pre-migration invoice without admission can be credited or repaired only when its database observation predates the closure cutoff, and it permanently blocks a positive cleanup certificate because its original ownership is unknown. A multi-mount host continues serving but cannot enter terminal cleanup or receive a positive certificate because its historical credit ledger cannot safely assign every row to one mount. Legacy unowned work and an ambiguous late payment also prevent certification. Tempo charge reservations persist before broadcast and remain unresolved until a matching durable payment marker is booked; an uncertain broadcast cannot be treated as unpaid. A used x402 or Tempo channel can support a positive certificate only when its original chain and contract binding and complete local history are known, its original claims, refunds, closes, and payout reports are discharged, and the configured RPC provides matching finalized chain state and receipts. The SDK checks that evidence against the same closed operation and unchanged local obligations before persisting or returning it. Missing historical provenance, including a channel table that predates history tracking even if it is empty, pending or ambiguous effects, and unavailable or conflicting chain evidence remain ineligible; an old zero-balance snapshot is insufficient. This is cooperative evidence about SDK-owned obligations, not proof that a hostile builder has no hidden liabilities. The platform must separately prove its supplier and wallet obligations before deleting a resource.

A hosted Tempo close stops cooperative attempts after three retired reservations (four broadcast attempts). Its final report obligation becomes `exhausted`, and its drain becomes `exit_required`. The caller receives HTTP 409 `tempo_channel_exit_required` and must use the timed on-chain exit. The debit and close intent remain intact: restoring spendable credit while an old close might still land could pay the same balance twice. An exhausted obligation remains watched for chain finalization. A late receipt or recovered cooperative close claims that terminal row and sends the payout report once; a confirmed payer exit resolves it to `no_report`. Cleanup remains blocked until that evidence arrives.

Missing, malformed, mismatched, or conflicting channel descriptors also make the registered drain terminal `exit_required`, before reserving a report or broadcasting. The catalogued `tempo_session_close_descriptor_refused` event carries the channel, the caller's amount at stake in native token units and currency micro-units, and the refusal. Sending a corrected descriptor under that drain ID returns the same timed-exit instruction without another debit or broadcast. Its funds remain in the channel, the close intent prevents reuse, and the caller recovers them through the timed exit. A concurrent close that already landed can still fulfill the drain from chain evidence.

A definitive search that exhausts the reservation-time range or reaches block zero without evidence makes the report obligation terminal `unmatched`. Automatic report-history searches and unproven loss-history discovery for that same hosted close stop until the explicit retry. Finalized spending restrictions and pending loss classification remain intact. A matching known transaction or verified receipt can still recover the refund and loss classification; a separate unresolved caller refund can still be checked by the drain sweep. `tempo_session_close_recovery_unmatched` carries the channel, obligation, and potential builder payout in native token units. This state continues to block cleanup with `settlement_reports_unresolved`: terminal search failure is not proof that the builder received no payment, so deleting the report history would risk losing its payout report. `tempo_session_close_attempts_exhausted` also carries the channel and potential payout. Both events use decimal strings for native amounts. Searches paused at their per-pass budget keep their existing cursor and continue on the next poll.

After repairing incomplete RPC history, explicitly reopen an unmatched report search with the supported server function. Use the original deployment identity and the obligation ID from the event; the database pool is the same pool configured for that deployment:

```ts
import { retryHostedTempoCloseReport } from "@dvmkit/sdk/server";

const reopened = await retryHostedTempoCloseReport({
  db, dvmId, scope, obligationId,
});
```

The function returns `true` only when it changes an owned, unsubmitted `unmatched` or `no_report` close obligation back to `reserved` and clears its search cursor. The next session sweep or hosting closure check searches from the chain head. It does not broadcast or reopen the caller's drain. An active, submitted, delivered, foreign, or competing report is not reopened. Use the authenticated admin route below for remote operation; direct calls require deployment database access.

When escrow history is permanently unavailable, the operator can explicitly forgo an owned, unsubmitted `unmatched` builder payout report:

```ts
import { writeOffHostedTempoCloseReport } from "@dvmkit/sdk/server";

const writtenOff = await writeOffHostedTempoCloseReport({
  db, dvmId, scope, obligationId, note: "Escrow history is irrecoverable",
});
```

This returns `true` only for the transition from `unmatched` to `no_report`; every other state, submitted report, foreign identity, and repeat call returns `false`. The note is required and retained in `close_write_off_note`. `tempo_session_close_report_written_off` carries the channel and `amount_forgone_native`, the builder payout being abandoned in native token units. This discharges that report's cleanup blocker; it does not discharge caller refund liabilities, move money, or declare a refund paid. Use it only after deciding to abandon the missing builder report. If that written-off close lands later, its payout is not reported: the recorded write-off forgoes it. `retryHostedTempoCloseReport` can reopen the written-off `no_report` row before that close lands; restore complete history and reopen it before the late landing if the payout should still be reported.

### Authenticated close-report administration

Hosted deployments expose these routes beside the Tempo drain routes. They use the same signed admin challenge and current or grace-period builder lock keys as `/admin/credit/tempo-drains`. Authentication runs before dependency checks or request validation. Without hosted settlement reports configured, authenticated requests return HTTP 503 with `error: "tempo_close_report_unavailable"`.

- `GET /admin/credit/tempo-close-reports?limit=20` defaults to owned, unsubmitted `unmatched` Tempo close obligations with an available action. Completed `no_report` rows stay out of this queue, including retired attempts, resolved payer exits, and completed write-offs. Use `?all=1` for an audit view of both terminal states, including submitted rows and rows without an available action. The default limit is 20, with a maximum of 50; pass the opaque `next_after` as `after` using the same audit flag for the next page. `all` accepts `0` or `1`. Timestamps are Unix milliseconds. Amounts are decimal strings in native token units, never floating-point numbers.
- `POST /admin/credit/retry-tempo-close-report` takes `{ "obligation_id": "close-report-test" }` and returns `{ "status": "reserved", "obligation_id": "close-report-test" }`. Retry accepts only `unmatched` and `no_report`, with no submitted transaction or competing report for the channel. It clears the durable search cursor; the next sweep searches again without broadcasting or changing the caller's drain. A successful transition emits `tempo_session_close_report_reopened` with `obligation_id`, `channel_id`, `prior_status`, `written_off` (whether a prior write-off decision existed), and `amount_native`. Refused retries emit no reopened event.
- `POST /admin/credit/write-off-tempo-close-report` takes `{ "obligation_id": "close-report-test", "note": "History irrecoverable" }`. A note of 1–2000 characters after trimming is required. Success returns `{ "status": "written_off", "obligation_id": "close-report-test", "channel_id": "0x…", "amount_forgone_native": "9007199254740993", "note": "History irrecoverable" }`. Only an unsubmitted `unmatched` report can be written off. The decimal amount is the reserved highest voucher less the amount already settled, in native token units. This abandons a builder payout report and emits `tempo_session_close_report_written_off` with its channel and amount; it never pays or releases caller money.

The list response is `{ "reports": [{ "obligation_id": "close-report-test", "channel_id": "0x…", "amount_native": "9007199254740993", "status": "unmatched", "created_at_ms": 1000, "updated_at_ms": 2000, "delivered_at_ms": null, "retryable": true, "write_off_allowed": true, "written_off": false, "write_off_note": null, "written_off_at_ms": null }], "next_after": "opaque-cursor" }`. `next_after` is omitted when the page is shorter than the limit. `delivered_at_ms` is nullable and does not imply a terminal report was delivered.

Each row describes the actions the store would accept at that read:

- `retryable` uses the retry transition's exact predicate: an unsubmitted `unmatched` or `no_report` row with no competing close report for the same deployment and channel. Another unsubmitted `no_report` row does not compete. A reserved, observed, delivered, or otherwise unresolved successor makes a retired attempt non-retryable.
- `write_off_allowed` uses the write-off transition's exact predicate: an unsubmitted `unmatched` row. It can be true even when retry is blocked by a competing report.
- `written_off` means a write-off note is retained. `write_off_note` and `written_off_at_ms` contain that decision's note and database timestamp, or `null` when absent. Retry retains this history; the flag does not mean a reopened search is still discharged.

The audit view lets an operator explicitly reconsider a write-off or an incorrectly inferred payer exit. A solitary `no_report` row can be `retryable: true`, although it stays outside the default queue because its previous outcome is complete. A retired attempt beside a delivered successor has both action flags false. These flags describe a snapshot: a concurrent transition can still cause HTTP 409 when an action is submitted. No available action is inferred merely from the status name.

Both POST routes return HTTP 409 with `{ "error": "tempo_close_report_conflict", "message": "…", "obligation_id": "close-report-test" }` when the store refuses the transition, including repeat write-offs. An absent obligation, another deployment's obligation, or an obligation for another operation returns HTTP 404 with `tempo_close_report_not_found`. Both POST routes trim `obligation_id` before lookup, mutation, logging, and response. Invalid JSON, IDs, or notes return HTTP 400 with `invalid_request`; invalid page queries return HTTP 400 with `invalid_query`. HTTP errors use the existing `{ error, message }` shape, and unauthenticated requests return HTTP 401. Portable request and response examples live in `protocol-fixtures/tempo-close-reports.json`.

The response types `TempoCloseReportSummary`, `TempoCloseReportsResponse`, `RetryTempoCloseReportResponse`, and `WriteOffTempoCloseReportResponse` are exported from `@dvmkit/sdk/server`. The direct `writeOffHostedTempoCloseReport` call retains its boolean return by default; pass `includeDetails: true` to receive `{ channelId, amountForgoneNative }` on success or `false` on refusal. `HostedTempoCloseReportWriteOff` describes that success result.

The session sweep and hosting closure reconciliation also finish `exit_required` drains without another caller poll. A cooperative close requires its receipt's refund amount. A payer exit requires a finalized empty channel, matching payer-exit history, and retained pre-finalization collateral; the collateral less settled vouchers is a lower bound on the payer refund. The bound must cover the exact drain debit. The drain becomes `sent` together with its durable drain report, and its settlement evidence records `terminal_outcome: "payer_exit"` or `"cooperative_close"`.

Before recording permanent backing loss, every poll, recovery, and settlement sweep resolves how the same channel finalized. A verified cooperative refund offsets the unpaid voucher headroom: loss is `max(0, highest voucher - settled to payee - refunded to payer)`. A covering refund leaves the credit status unchanged and emits `tempo_session_drain_finalized` with zero builder loss. A partial refund can leave a real residual loss; a proven payer exit retains only unpaid successfully consumed service: `max(0, consumed - settled to payee)`. Unused voucher authority is not earned builder value. Successful terminal draw settlement advances that consumed amount; voucher acceptance alone does not. A positive loss makes the credit `unbacked`, the outcome gains `_unbacked`, and `tempo_session_drain_unbacked_resolved` reports the retained residual. Caller refund and payee settlement remain separate amounts.

Unavailable receipts or incomplete history defer permanent loss classification. The channel still becomes finalized, so its balance cannot fund another draw. Its existing channel state retains `creditLossPending` and, for a paused search, `creditLossSearchBefore`; ordinary sweeps retry it even after finalization, except when its matching hosted close is terminal `unmatched` without a known transaction and requires the explicit report retry above. An operator refund decision, an exact drain amount without its matching close, or `refund_payment_proven` alone cannot supply same-close evidence. This prevents new false loss records; it does not reverse existing losses when an operator later pays independently.

Ledger initialization adds nullable `tempo_credit_losses.refunded_to_payer_native`, `loss_native`, and `loss_basis` columns. Existing rows retain null, meaning their exact refund and residual were not recorded; initialization invents no zero or historical correction. New cooperative SDK classifications retain the amounts and their basis without changing `settled_on_chain_native`. Payer-exit history proves the unpaid consumed-service loss, but its refund bound is not an exact receipt amount, so the exact refund field stays null. `GET /admin/credit/tempo-losses` adds decimal-string-or-null `refunded_to_payer_native` and `loss_native`, plus `loss_basis` (`cooperative_close`, `payer_exit`, or `caller_asserted`, with null for historical rows); the portable examples are in [tempo-credit-losses.json](../protocol-fixtures/tempo-credit-losses.json). `CreditLedger.listTempoCreditLosses` exposes optional nullable bigint `refundedToPayerNative` and `lossNative` fields plus optional nullable `lossBasis`, and the version 1 financial export includes the exact decimal strings while remaining compatible with schemas lacking these columns. Upgrade every SDK writer sharing this ledger to obtain the corrected automatic classification. A direct `terminalizeTempoCredits` call asserts permanent loss. Its optional `closeKind` selects the verified cooperative receipt or proven payer-exit calculation; the runtime supplies it only after reading matching chain evidence. `lossNative` always describes classified builder loss, and `lossBasis` records which calculation produced it. Its optional `refundedToPayerNative` accepts only a trusted same-close exact amount; when both new evidence fields are omitted, the existing caller-asserted `highest voucher - settled` loss behavior remains and records null for the exact refund and `lossBasis: "caller_asserted"`, rather than asserting zero. That omission is never the SDK runtime's substitute for missing close evidence. Custom loss writers remain responsible for their trusted permanent-loss assertions.

Custom ledgers may advertise `readonly tempoCreditLossEvidenceVersion: 1` only when they implement both close calculations, retain actual payee settlement, distinguish exact refund from unknown, apply the permanent mutation atomically through its supplied transaction, and use that transaction for loss diagnostic reads. Both SDK ledgers advertise version 1. A verified zero loss never invokes a permanent mutator. Positive loss on a ledger without version 1 remains pending and returns a retry before drain completion or terminal metadata; there is no legacy mutation fallback. Finalized spending restrictions and sweep retries remain. Channel-only recovery also retains trusted exact refund or positive payer-exit payment evidence on the matching open drain before classification or capability deferral. The drain must match the channel and its credit binding; a close intent selects its drain, and an unassociated close requires one unique open drain. Positive payment observations for the matched credit and drain are saved atomically with finalized restrictions and retained collateral, before per-drain proof writes. Operator RELEASE checks that sticky observation under the same channel lock, including after a proof-write failure; a later zero cannot erase it. Unknown proof still permits the documented operator assertion of nonpayment, and exact zero remains distinct from a positive observation. An immutable operator decision that commits while proof is still unknown wins over a later observation. An unavailable or failed monetary evidence writer defers that channel's classification and drain completion while finalized restrictions and retry visibility persist. The sweep reports incomplete work through `inFlight`, logs `tempo_session_close_reconcile_failed`, and continues protecting other channels; a failed protective channel commit aborts before classification and makes no durable payment claim. The SDK memory ledger implements these proof writers for development accounting, without durable operator decisions. Upgrading the ledger resumes that same pending classification. Loss recovery searches from the channel creation time, or the beginning of history when that time is unavailable, and retains paused search progress. A completed unmatched loss-search pass clears its cursor so a later pass can revisit newly available history. Confirming a channel reference later cannot move this search past the close. `listTempoCreditLosses` accepts optional `channelId` and transaction arguments. The runtime independently filters returned rows to the exact channel; an unadvertised custom diagnostic reader runs after release of the channel transaction, because it may ignore the supplied transaction and acquire its own connection.

Missing close history, unavailable collateral, and a short refund leave the drain open and emit `tempo_session_drain_refund_unproven` with the channel, caller amount at stake, and reason. Each distinct reason emits once per drain; warning claims are retained in the channel snapshot across restarts and shared-store replicas. Transient RPC failures and failed accounting/report writes leave it open and emit `tempo_session_close_reconcile_failed` instead; they do not declare a refund unproven. Older finalized rows without a retained collateral snapshot need independent receipt evidence; empty state alone cannot discharge them. The sweep never restores or debits the credit again.

Both SDK ledgers implement `requireTempoDrainExit`. A custom ledger without it receives the explicit timed-exit HTTP 409 with `status: "pending"` instead of a server error, but cannot persist the refusal or participate in automatic terminal drain recovery. Its response permits a retry with a corrected credential when cooperative attempts remain; that retry can broadcast, and keeps the original debit. Implement that capability for the same durable behavior; the fallback keeps the original debit and never broadcasts another close after hosted exhaustion.

The same retry can repair an incorrectly inferred payer exit. `tempo_session_close_exit_resolved` is inferred from the latest uncancelled payer close request after its grace period, because withdrawal has no separate event. An RPC node that omits a cooperative close during that period can cause a false `no_report` result. Restore complete logs before reopening the report. Close-report administration also adds the nullable `close_written_off_at_ms` column during initialization. New write-offs record their decision time independently of later retries. Existing notes remain readable, with a null timestamp when the original decision time was not recorded; `updated_at_ms` is not used to invent that time.

Upgrading adds `exhausted` and `unmatched` to the report-state constraint and the nullable `close_write_off_note` column without changing existing rows. Initialization compares the constraint definition and only replaces it when necessary; later boots retain the same constraint without another validation scan. The session snapshot retains `depositBeforeFinalization` when it observes finalization; `exit_required` is an additional drain status. Upgrade every instance sharing these stores before serving terminal drains with the new status.

### Held Tempo drain decisions

A held drain can be completed with signed admin authentication even when its receipt or payer-exit refund cannot be proven. Both routes require a durable ledger and its Tempo session store. They accept `credit_id`, `drain_id`, a required `reason` of 1–2000 characters after trimming, and optional `evidence` of 1–4000 characters after trimming. IDs are matched exactly. Other fields, including partial amounts, are refused.

- `POST /admin/credit/book-tempo-drain` attests that the **whole held amount** has been returned to the caller, including any remainder paid off-path. It changes `pending` or `exit_required` to `sent` and enqueues the existing drain report in the same transaction. A hosted deployment (a platform URL is configured) requires the actual transactional reporter; missing wiring returns 503 before a new decision. Standalone deployments retain optional reporting. Booking also clears the drain's old write-off annotation transactionally; separate channel close-report write-offs remain unchanged. Its `sent_ref` contains only `source: "operator"` and `channel_id`; it does not invent a transaction hash, chain refund amount, signed receipt, or verified-close repair metadata.
- `POST /admin/credit/release-tempo-drain` records that the refund was not paid. It changes either held status to `released` and restores the exact recorded debit, rail remainders, and funding-lot debits. It sends no paid-drain report and changes no channel close-report obligation. Existing credit restrictions, expiry, channel closure, and finalization still apply. The route never restores channel collateral or reopens a channel.

A partial payment justifies neither decision. Book only after independently verifying that the full amount, including any off-path remainder, was returned. Use release only when no refund was paid. These routes do not allocate partial refunds. A new release returns HTTP 409 `tempo_drain_close_pending` while this drain's close may already have been broadcast and the channel is not finalized. For a matching close intent on a non-final channel, release requires `forecast_pending` to be exactly true, no saved transaction hash, and no `settled_before_close` snapshot. A nonnull snapshot, including zero, is written just before broadcast; receipt timeout can leave no saved hash. Legacy intents default to `forecast_pending: false` and may already have broadcast even with both newer markers null. Current intents whose forecast completed also become false, so they conservatively refuse release even if broadcast has not started yet; use the existing close/exit resolution. A new reservation whose forecast remains true, or no matching intent, can still be released if all other guards permit it. A valid last-attempt settlement snapshot is required before every broadcast; retries cannot replace it with unknown data. An open channel snapshot, elapsed time, or retired hosted report reservation does not prove non-payment. Reconcile the final outcome first. A retained positive refund or positive-payment fact returns HTTP 409 `tempo_drain_refund_paid`; independently return any remaining amount and book the whole refund instead. A historical `refund_short` refusal without an exact retained amount returns HTTP 409 `tempo_drain_refund_unknown`; recover the original evidence before asserting non-payment. Genuine matching decision replays run before these new eligibility checks.

Both responses contain `status` (`sent` or `released`), `credit_id`, `drain_id`, `channel_id`, `amount_micro`, `amount_native`, `currency`, `decision`, `balance_micro`, `spendable_micro`, and `replayed`. `decision` records `{ source: "operator", decision: "book_refunded" | "release", reason, evidence?, decidedAt }`; the time is Unix milliseconds. `amount_micro` and nullable `amount_native` are the original held debit, not a new payment quote. `balance_micro` is the current ledger balance. `spendable_micro` also applies credit status, expiry, and the channel's closing/finalized gate. Restored balance can therefore remain unavailable for new spending.

A replay with the same action, trimmed reason, and evidence returns the original decision with `replayed: true`. Changing any of these arguments, choosing the opposite decision, or deciding an automatically booked drain returns HTTP 409 `drain_decision_conflict`. A released drain cannot later be automatically booked; an operator booking cannot produce another drain report. Missing credits or drains return HTTP 404, incompatible rails return HTTP 422 `drain_not_repairable`, malformed requests return HTTP 400 `invalid_body`, and a ledger without durable decision support returns HTTP 503 `tempo_drain_decision_unavailable`. Authentication runs first and retains the existing HTTP 401 behavior.

`GET /admin/credit/tempo-drains` now includes every held Tempo session drain, including legacy drain write-offs whose debit is still held. `held_reason` contains the latest established proof refusal (`close_evidence_missing`, `close_history_unmatched`, `collateral_missing`, or `refund_short`); without one it reports `exit_required` or `refund_unproven`. Retryable RPC or report-write errors do not establish a new reason. `close_reports` shows related terminal close-report obligations with their IDs, statuses, and retained write-off flags. A report write-off never resolves the caller's drain. `include_resolved=true` additionally includes terminal operator decisions, whose `held_reason` is null and whose `decision` remains readable. `proven_refund_native` is a nullable decimal string: null means unknown, `"0"` is proven zero, and a positive value is retained payment evidence. Receipt proof paths persist the greatest exact observed refund before a floor refusal or report transaction can roll back. Payer-exit calculations are lower bounds, so they never populate the exact amount. A positive lower bound sets the separate sticky boolean `refund_payment_proven`; a nonpositive bound proves no exact amount and retains the short-refund refusal. False means no positive-payment fact is retained, not that no refund was paid. A later missing read, label change, zero lower bound, or exact zero observation cannot erase a prior positive-payment fact or exact positive amount. An unknown historical short-refund label also remains until exact evidence is recovered. Existing pagination and evidence-read controls apply. The admin read explicitly includes write-offs; background payout snapshots keep their existing write-off filter. Custom `listChannelDrains` implementations can use `includeWrittenOff: true` for the same admin view, while `includeResolved` retains audit semantics.

A successful new decision emits `tempo_drain_booked_as_refunded` or `tempo_drain_released`, with the channel, drain, credit, original amount in currency micro-units, and nullable native amount. Every decision event also carries `drain_reference`, a safe opaque correlation value: `sha256:` followed by lowercase SHA-256 hex of the UTF-8 encoding of `JSON.stringify([credit_id, drain_id])`. It is a digest, not the original identifier. It remains present when strict diagnostic policy omits unusual caller-chosen raw IDs. Replays emit no second event. Reasons and evidence remain in the durable audit record and authenticated listing; diagnostics omit them. Portable examples are in [tempo-drain-decisions.json](../protocol-fixtures/tempo-drain-decisions.json).

Initialization adds nullable `credit_drains.operator_decision`, `hold_reason`, and `proven_refund_native NUMERIC(78,0)` columns, plus `refund_payment_proven BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE`. Existing rows acquire no invented decision or historical proof reason. The version 1 financial export retains only operator source, action, and decision time from the audit object, alongside the drain's existing amounts and timestamps and decimal-string `proven_refund_native` and boolean `refund_payment_proven`; it excludes free-text reasons, evidence, and unknown nested fields. Older schemas without these columns remain exportable. Upgrade every process sharing this ledger before enabling decisions. Older automatic writers do not understand the decision audit, although terminal status guards continue to prevent another booking. The supported server types are `TempoDrainDecision`, `TempoDrainDecisionArgs`, `TempoDrainDecisionResult`, and `TempoDrainHoldReason`; custom durable ledgers may implement operator decisions and cosmetic held-reason persistence. Completing held Tempo drains requires the exact-refund-evidence and positive-payment writers, even when the ledger advertises loss evidence version 1. A direct `CreditLedger.decideTempoDrain` caller owns its drain-report transaction and enqueue; hosted callers must provide both. `recordTempoDrainRefund` accepts only trusted exact receipt-derived evidence, never an operator request override or payer-exit lower bound. `recordTempoDrainPayment` retains trusted proof that some refund was paid without claiming an exact amount. Exact positive observations also set that sticky fact. Existing rows receive no invented payment proof; the default false only means no retained positive fact. Failed monetary evidence writes abort completion and emit `tempo_drain_refund_evidence_write_failed`. A cosmetic held-reason write failure emits `tempo_drain_hold_reason_write_failed` while preserving the original definitive refusal and any retained monetary proof. The development memory ledger does not provide operator decisions.
